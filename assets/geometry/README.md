# Finite celestial solids

The 70 named bodies in `derivation.json` share bounded, immutable geometry at both LODs, in selected and neighbour rendering, and in CPU picking. Seven solids retain broad source-model concavity; the other sixty-three have **authored**, displaced ice/rock/comet relief. This is a visualization, not a height measurement service. Small shading features, colour, lighting, orientation, scale, jets and orbits remain illustrative. No source texture is registered to these meshes; Viking and Dawn maps retain their earlier provenance and missing-area handling.

All meshes are uniformly fitted **inside their existing display ellipsoids**, preserving navigation distance bounds. Source UV seams are welded; broad concavities are retained through simplification to at most 16,384 faces. The sixteen additional small worlds include authored red Jovian shoulders and basins, cratered co-orbital moons, powder-soft Trojan moons, ring-neighbour grooves, an unequal dog-bone asteroid and small companions, plus a nearly round main-belt world. Scientific context follows [NASA’s moon pages](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/) and ESO’s [Kleopatra](https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2113/) and [Hygiea](https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso1918/) observations. These new solids are entirely authored; no new mission shape, surface map or measured crater placement was acquired. The nine additional remote worlds include authored equatorial skirts, deep sponge-like basins and flattened contact lobes. These are image-informed artistic forms, not acquired mission meshes. Chariklo and Quaoar rings use a separate shared analytic plane, with enhanced widths and compressed distances; the surface atlas does not include ring faces.

The dense cohort retains up to 16,384 faces for sixty-eight shapes; Eros and Bennu keep their native, lower-resolution inputs. Authored forms are sampled more finely before simplification, so basin walls, equatorial flanges and rough limbs retain more geometry. Kleopatra uses a closed longitudinal loft for its unequal lobes and narrow bridge, avoiding the long angular faces of its earlier radial illustration. Its shape remains entirely authored. This refinement adds no measured geography or registered textures.

Eight outer moons join the Pluto, Eris, Haumea and Makemake families. NASA’s [Pluto moon pages](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/pluto/moons/facts/) inform Styx’s narrow outline, Nix’s pale body and reddish region, Kerberos’ unequal joined lobes and Hydra’s irregular shoulders. These are authored image-informed forms, not mission meshes or registered maps. NASA’s [Dysnomia observation](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/hubble-view-of-eris-and-dysnomia/), [Haumea overview](https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/solar_system_level2/haumea.html) and [MK 2 discovery](https://science.nasa.gov/image-detail/image1-_p1618aw/) establish companions and relationships; their detailed shapes and surfaces remain unresolved here. Kerberos uses a closed unequal-lobe loft. The eight newly authored moons retain geometry-based sunlight visibility with a bounded, softer grazing-angle material handoff, to reduce hard triangular dark patches from tiny near-horizon facets. Front-lit deep hollows and joined-lobe occlusion remain visible; this shading is artistic, not measured photometry. Ice regions, pits, fine grain, dark materials, Nix’s red-region placement and all displayed orbits are artistic hypotheses. 

Twelve further trans-Neptunian worlds and companions share this atlas. Their profiles are authored, including Orcus/Vanth, Gonggong/Xiangliu, Salacia/Actaea, Varda/Ilmarë, Varuna, Ixion, Weywot and 2002 MS4. Research links in their bilingual guide notes justify spectral, light-curve, occultation or mutual-orbit context; none supplies a complete resolved surface. Varuna’s elongated proportions are inspired by a light-curve interpretation; Varda’s flattening is illustrative. 2002 MS4’s broad depression is inspired by an occultation limb study, with invented placement and detailed form. Large body-fixed colour regions, wide basins, shoulders and filtered regolith persist at both LODs. Basin slopes and colour regions share the mesh’s normalized ellipsoid coordinates; authored incoming meridians expose the broad hollows and elongated shoulders. Fine shading and colour grain remain subordinate to these structures. Rounded primary worlds have gentler broad shape variation than their irregular companions. 

The two authored longitudinal lofts use gentle spatial relief displaced along their own surface normals; finer regolith remains in the filtered material. This keeps the narrow bridge from folding into stretched radial bands during local inspection. The same gentle relief range now applies to all sixty-three authored shapes: finite facets retain broad basins, shoulders, flanges and rubble, with finer grain in the filtered material. The seven source-derived shapes and two previously refined lofts retain their exact position/normal and normalized BVH bits. All seventy keep the same identities, two LODs, CPU picking and outside navigation limits. No new source model or measured geography is introduced.

Dense authored roughness uses deterministic irregular spatial fields rather than crossed periodic waves. Simplification removes reverse-winding duplicate flaps and validates closed topology and finite used normals before publication. Required source files must exist before generation; missing inputs fail instead of silently producing authored replacements.

Authored crater basins and stone mounds change actual geometry; their locations and depths are not scientific observations. `tools/derive_solid_geometry.py` creates the data with offline `numpy` and `fast-simplification`; neither is a browser/runtime dependency. Reproduction inputs, original hashes, pages and model labels are retained in ignored round-68 and round-84 acquisition artifacts; output hashes and counts are in `derivation.json`.

The four binary files total **53,743,872 bytes**. They load once into two shared GPU storage buffers and a matching CPU atlas, independent of star count and quality. Quality changes reuse these solids; renderer destruction frees GPU buffers. LOD adds filtered material detail, not a different opaque shape. Exact float32 position/normal pairs are shared through indexed faces; normal seams and all source/artist shape bits are preserved. GPU traversal visits nearer branches first and prunes farther branches against visible depth, using a bounded traversal stack; the measured integer-path variant increased solid-world peak cost and was removed. Each shape has at most 8,191 nodes; CPU picking follows independent escape links through the same four-face leaves. The matching CPU atlas stays indexed during rendering and picking; existing diagnostic readers can request an expanded face view lazily. This changes storage, not the observed terrain. The original finite analytic shapes remain only as a synchronous pure-model fallback before initialization; the visible renderer awaits complete geometry. Missing or incomplete geometry produces the existing visible initialization failure.

Each binary also has a deterministic gzip transfer copy, totalling **27,737,598 bytes**, a 48.39% reduction from the raw atlas. Both compressed and decoded sizes/hashes are verified before accepting the atlas; streaming decode writes into a fixed destination bounded by the declared raw size. Environments without gzip decompression support use the retained raw binaries. GPU/CPU geometry bits and the two storage buffers remain identical. Independent image decode, geometry validation and render pipeline creation overlap during startup; the scene appears only after all are ready, and partial GPU resources are released after an initialization failure. This changes transfer and scheduling, not surface fidelity or per-frame work.
